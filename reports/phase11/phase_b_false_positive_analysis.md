## 3. Repeated Failure Archetypes

Detailed examination of the false positives identified five distinct failure patterns:

### Archetype 1: Shared Hosting Platforms & Free Website Builders ($p \approx 0.95 - 1.00$)
* **Examples:**
  * `https://ardmadurai.weebly.com/` ($p = 1.00$)
  * `https://connecticutsac.wixsite.com/sac1746` ($p = 1.00$)
* **Mechanism:** In the training dataset, multi-tenant hosting platforms are overwhelmingly populated with phishing attacks (`weebly.com` has 1,954 phishing URLs vs. 10 legitimate URLs; `wixsite.com` has 117 phishing vs. 7 legitimate). Consequently, trees split on `dom_len = 10` or `11`, `is_https = 1`, and land directly in pure phishing leaf nodes.

### Archetype 2: Hyphenated Domain Names ($p \approx 0.90 - 1.00$)
* **Examples:**
  * `https://green-technology.org/` ($p = 1.00$)
  * `https://star-center.org/` ($p = 1.00$)
  * `https://mifne-autism.com/` ($p = 0.99$)
  * `https://sci-monitoring.com/` ($p = 0.97$)
  * `https://nke-instrumentation.com/` ($p = 0.97$)
  * `https://afghan-institute.org/` ($p = 0.95$)
* **Mechanism:** The vast majority of legitimate domains in the training set are single-word dictionary terms without hyphens (mean `dash_cnt = 0.24`). Attackers frequently use hyphenated keywords to spoof brands. When an authentic non-profit or research entity registers a hyphenated domain, the model flags it as an impersonation attempt.

### Archetype 3: URLs with Dynamic Tracking, Session, or Analytics Parameters ($p \approx 0.85 - 0.95$)
* **Examples:**
  * `https://healthcare.ascension.org/Locations/Michigan/MIDET/Southfield-Ascension-Providence-Hospital-Southfield-Campus?_ga=2.142884275.787900410.1570527361-558817569.1570527361` ($p = 0.95$, `query_len = 57`, `entropy = 5.15`, 48 digits)
  * `http://www.mikon.eu/eng/index.htm?1443697208248` ($p = 0.58$, timestamp parameter with 13 digits)
* **Mechanism:** Long alphanumeric query tokens inflate Shannon entropy and digit ratios, mimicking obfuscated credential harvesting kits.

### Archetype 4: Institutional & Municipal URLs with Alphanumeric Path IDs ($p \approx 0.85 - 0.96$)
* **Examples:**
  * `https://www.wilcotx.gov/275/Conservation-Foundation` ($p = 0.96$, Williamson County, Texas government portal)
  * `https://www.uab.cat/web/fundacio-salut-i-envelliment-1345754058073.html` ($p = 0.93$, Autonomous University of Barcelona)
* **Mechanism:** Content Management Systems (CMS) embed database primary keys and publication timestamps into URL slugs. Decision trees interpret digits inside paths as malicious entropy.

### Archetype 5: Borderline False Positives ($p \in [0.50, 0.60)$)
* **Examples:**
  * `https://52north.org` ($p = 0.58$, spatial research lab)
  * `https://walk21.com` ($p = 0.58$, international conference on walking)
  * `http://lafayettepubliclibrary.org` ($p = 0.58$, public library)
* **Mechanism:** These represent 116 borderline decisions where 50 to 59 decision trees voted for phishing due to slightly elevated lengths or digits in the domain name.

## 5. Dataset Label Uncertainty vs. Model Prediction Errors

When analyzing false positives in cybersecurity datasets, researchers must evaluate whether the errors reflect genuine model failure or **ground-truth label noise**:

### 1. High-Confidence Authentic Benign Entities (>90% of FPs)
* Government institutions (`.gov`), accredited universities (`.edu`, `.ac.in`, `.cat`), established health providers (Ascension, Alberta Health, Calvary Hospital), and recognized enterprises.
* **Finding:** These are undeniable model prediction errors caused by structural feature overlap (hyphens, digits, HTTPS, path depth). Their benign labels in the dataset are fully accurate.

### 2. Ambiguous Hosting Entities (~5% - 8% of FPs)
* URLs hosted on free platforms like `weebly.com` or `wixsite.com` (e.g., `https://ardmadurai.weebly.com/`, `https://connecticutsac.wixsite.com/sac1746`).
* *Nuance:* In open threat-intelligence feeds, free hosting subdomains are frequently repurposed or hijacked. While these specific links appear benign in content, their presence in a dataset where 99% of identical hostnames are malicious creates severe label friction.
* **Conclusion:** The false-positive issue in PhishGuardAI is **primarily an architectural model and representation problem**, not a symptom of widespread label corruption in the dataset.

## 6. Evidentiary Distinctions

* **Verified Facts from Actual Files:** The production model produces exactly 324 false positives via `model.predict()` (331 via $p \ge 0.50$) on the 23,047 test samples.
* **Measured Results:** 90.33% of false positives are HTTPS URLs, compared to only 33.82% of true negatives. Digit count is 10× higher in false positives.
* **Hypotheses Needing Further Testing:** Post-processing probability calibration (e.g., Platt scaling or isotonic regression) will pull the 116 borderline false positives ($0.50 \le p < 0.60$) below the 0.50 decision threshold without hurting high-confidence phishing recall.
* **Limitations:** Ground truth evaluation relies on the original labels of `Dataset.csv`; live web validation of archived URLs was intentionally omitted in accordance with offline audit protocols.
